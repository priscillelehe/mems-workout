How your week works

Start with 2 gym days a week: one glutes & legs day and one upper body day. Once that feels easy to fit in, add the second glute day for 3 days. Later, when lifting feels routine, you can switch to 4 days.

For your first two weeks, do 2 sets of every exercise, even when the plan says 3. There’s a switch for this on every workout. Learning the moves well matters more than doing lots of sets.

Leave at least one day between your two leg days.

Lifting and running together

Strength training is one of the best things you can do for your running. Strong glutes, hips and calves help you run more efficiently and handle the pounding of all those miles.

  • Avoid leg days the day before a long run, a hard interval session or a race.
  • An easy run and a gym session on the same day is fine. Run first, or leave a few hours between them.
  • Race week: skip the leg days or do one light session early in the week.
  • Upper body days barely affect your running, so put them wherever they fit.

Warming up

Walk or jog easily on the treadmill for 5–8 minutes. Then do one light practice set of your first exercise, about 8 reps with half the weight, before your real sets.

Picking the right weight

The starting weights are just a guide. The right weight is one where you finish each set feeling you could do 2 or 3 more reps with good form.

  • Could easily do 5 more? Go up.
  • Form falling apart, or couldn’t finish? Go down. No shame in that at all.

Write the weight you used in the box under each exercise. It saves on your phone, so next time you know where you left off.

Getting stronger over time

When you can do the top number of reps on every set with good form, go up by the smallest step next time: 1–2 kg on dumbbells, or one plate on the cable machine. Your reps will drop a little, then you build them back up. Repeat. That slow climb is exactly what grows your glutes and arms.

Strength improves within a few weeks. Visible changes usually take a couple of months of showing up consistently, so trust the process.

Resting between sets

About 2 minutes after squats and deadlifts, 60–90 seconds after most other exercises, and 45 seconds for core. When you tick a set, the rest timer starts on its own.

Using the cable machine
  • Pulley height: pull the pin on the side of each pulley and slide it up or down. Low for kickbacks, abductions and curls. Chest height for chest presses and Pallof presses. High for pulldowns, pushdowns and face pulls.
  • Attachments: clip the rope, bar, handle or ankle strap onto the clip at the end of the cable.
  • Weight: push the pin fully into the weight stack. Each side has its own stack.
  • The numbers on a cable stack don’t match dumbbell kilos, so go by feel rather than the number.

The first time, ask a staff member to show you. It takes two minutes and makes everything easier.

Food, sleep and recovery

Running plus lifting uses a lot of energy, and your muscles only grow if you give them enough to build with. Eat regular meals with some protein in each one (eggs, yoghurt, meat, fish, tofu, beans, cheese), especially on days you train. Sleep matters just as much, because that’s when your muscles actually recover.

Soreness vs pain

Feeling sore a day or two after a workout is normal, especially in the first weeks. It gets much better once your body gets used to it. Sharp pain, or pain in a joint, is different: stop that exercise and ask a coach to check your form.

What to buy
  • Ankle strap for the cable machine (around €15–20). You need this for glute kickbacks and hip abductions. Check it clips onto the Precor machine.
  • Mini resistance bands (optional, around €20). Nice for glute warm-ups, but not essential.
  • A water bottle and supportive trainers.

You don’t need gloves, a lifting belt or supplements to start.
